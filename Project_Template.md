1. What is the problem I am solving?  The Core Problem: I am solving the crisis of unpredictable farming seasons in Kenya caused by climate change. For generations, smallholder farmers in our country’s breadbasket regions (like Uasin Gishu, Nakuru, and Trans Nzoia) relied on trusted historical calendar habits to know exactly when to plant maize and beans. Today, those predictable cycles are completely broken. Rainy seasons start weeks late, stop abruptly, or dump a month’s worth of destructive water in a single weekend.
The Broken Process:Right now, farmers only have access to broad, generic weather forecasts (like "expect rain over the Rift Valley"). These forecasts don't provide local data or compare current conditions to past trends.
The Result: Farmers are guessing. They plant seeds at the wrong time out of habit. The seeds either rot in dry soil or get washed away by flash floods. This leads to massive financial losses for families and threatens our national food security.

2.  What tools did I use?   
I built an end-to-end data pipeline using Python and the PyData Ecosystem to clean, analyze, mathematically prove, and map out these climate risks:
Pandas: I used this to clean decades of chaotic satellite climate data. I engineered a custom metric called the Rainfall Deficit by subtracting actual monthly rainfall from a 30-year historical baseline to see exactly how much our weather is deviating from normal.GeoPandas: I used this to read official vector shapefiles (.shp) of Kenya’s 47 counties. This allowed me to merge my standard data tables with actual geography so I could build interactive, color-coded regional maps.SciPy (scipy.stats): I used this to give my project strong academic weight. Instead of just guessing that the climate is changing, I used Pearson correlation tests to calculate $p$-values. Because my $p$-value is lower than $0.05$, I can mathematically prove that Kenya's rising temperatures and shrinking planting windows are statistically significant trends.Matplotlib & Seaborn: I used these to create clean, dual-axis graphs that overlay rising temperature lines directly over dropping rainfall bars, making the climate stress instantly visible.Streamlit: I used this web framework to turn my backend Python code into a beautiful, interactive web application that anyone can use without needing to understand code.3. What insights did I discover? & What is my "Aha!" moment?My InsightsThe Volatility Trap: My data proves that while Kenya's total annual rainfall hasn't dropped drastically, the distribution of that rain has changed completely. The planting window has physically shrunk by about two weeks. The rain is compressed into short, violent bursts that cause floods and soil erosion rather than actually hydrating crops.The Geographic Shift: My maps revealed that traditional, high-yield agricultural zones (like Kiambu and Nakuru) are actually warming up and drying out faster than historically dry regions, catching local farming cooperatives completely off guard.My "Aha!" MomentThe standout moment of my project happens on my Streamlit dashboard. When a user selects a county like Trans Nzoia, the app doesn't just show a static, boring chart.The moment my Python backend calculates that the current month's Rainfall Deficit has crossed a dangerous threshold, the GeoPandas map dynamically triggers an Emergency Adaptation Alert. The map flags the county and outputs a clear, actionable command: "CRITICAL SOIL MOISTURE DEFICIT: Delay standard maize planting. Distribute short-cycle, drought-resistant seeds immediately." This moves my project beyond a simple dashboard and turns it into a real-world, predictive rescue tool.4. How does the community or a business benefit from my work?Because this is a Social Impact Project, the real value of my work is measured in saved money and protected livelihoods:For Farming Communities: By checking my dashboard before spending money on seeds, fertilizer, and tractor hire, an entire village can decide to delay planting by two weeks based on real data. This saves hundreds of families from losing their hard-earned savings to seed rot.For Agricultural Cooperatives: Instead of blindly sending standard seeds to local depots, suppliers can use my "Climate Anomaly Score" to see which counties are facing a dry spell. They can proactively stock early-maturing or drought-resistant seed variants weeks before the season fails, minimizing wasted inventory.For Micro-Finance and Crop Insurance Firms: Local lenders can use my statistical models to evaluate the true climate risk of a county before issuing farm loans, protecting themselves from a spike in loan defaults during drought years.1.
